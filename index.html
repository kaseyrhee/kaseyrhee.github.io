I am a Ph.D. candidate in Political Science at Stanford University. My research studies American elections and political behavior using large-scale administrative datasets and causal inference techniques. Specifically, I study how personal life, such as family disagreements about politics, marriage, and divorce, shapes partisanship and participation. I also study U.S. election administration, including how voter registration, ballot access, and list-maintenance policies influence participation and electoral outcomes. In a separate line of work, I use computer vision and text analysis to study how candidates present themselves visually in campaign advertising.

My work has been supported by the National Science Foundation Graduate Research Fellowship, a Magic Grant from the Brown Institute for Media Innovation, and an American Democracy Fellowship from Stanford's Institute for Research in the Social Sciences.

I am on the academic job market in 2026–27.
